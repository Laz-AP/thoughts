*Building story 01 · Video essay and sketch*

The Home Insurance Building is gone, but the site still matters. Designed by William Le Baron Jenney and begun in 1884, it rose ten stories and 138 feet, and it is often called the first skyscraper because its masonry skin was carried by an iron skeleton rather than by thick walls alone. Two floors were added in 1891, bringing it to twelve stories and about 180 feet.

## Why it matters

The idea that a frame could carry the weight, and the walls could simply hang from it, is what allowed buildings to keep growing taller. It changed how cities were built, and Chicago was where it happened first.

## What is there now

The building was demolished in 1931 to make way for the Field Building, a Marshall Field's project that replaced at least six buildings. The western section of the Field Building now occupies the site.
